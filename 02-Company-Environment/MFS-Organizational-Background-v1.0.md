## 1. Purpose

This document records the preliminary understanding of Meridian
Financial Services (MFS) for use in planning a simulated IT audit. It
provides business and organizational context to help identify relevant
processes, systems, information assets, and areas requiring further
inquiry.

This is an auditor-prepared working document based on fictional scenario
information. In a real engagement, the auditor would obtain and
corroborate information through interviews, organizational charts,
policies, process documentation, system inventories, walkthroughs, and
other appropriate sources.

## 2. Organization Overview

Meridian Financial Services is a fictional, privately held, mid-sized
financial services organization headquartered in Florida, United States.
It provides digital financial services to individual and small-business
customers.

MFS relies on information technology to support customer onboarding,
account servicing, financial transaction processing, recordkeeping,
reporting, and internal operations.

All organization details in this document are fictional assumptions
created solely for this educational portfolio. They are not statements
about a real company.

## 4. Business Services and Processes

The scenario assumes MFS performs the following activities:

-   Customer onboarding and identity verification.
-   Digital customer account management.
-   Financial transaction processing.
-   Customer support and account servicing.
-   Financial reporting and reconciliation.
-   Small-business financial services.

The auditor should obtain process narratives or conduct walkthroughs to
understand how each significant process operates, which systems support
it, and where key controls are performed.

## 7. Information and Data Categories

The scenario indicates that MFS may process or maintain the following
information:

**Customer information** - Names and contact details. - Account
identification information. - Customer verification records.

**Financial information** - Transaction records. - Account balances. -
Financial statements and reconciliation records.

**Employee information** - Personnel records. - Payroll information. -
Employment documentation.

**Technology and security information** - System configurations. - User
and privileged-access records. - Security logs. - Network and
architecture documentation.

The sensitivity, volume, location, retention requirements, and
applicable protection requirements for each data category have not yet
been validated. These matters should be examined during the engagement.

## 8. Preliminary Technology Context

For planning purposes, the fictional MFS scenario assumes a hybrid
technology environment that may include:

-   Windows-based employee workstations.
-   Centralized identity and access management.
-   Windows Server and directory services.
-   Cloud-hosted business applications.
-   Financial and customer databases.
-   Network firewalls and endpoint protection.
-   Centralized security monitoring and logging.
-   Backup and recovery services.

This is a preliminary enterprise scenario, not a verified inventory.
Specific products, configurations, system owners, hosting locations,
interfaces, and dependencies will be documented separately in the IT
Infrastructure and Asset Inventory workpapers.

The auditor's separate home lab (VirtualBox, Ubuntu/Wazuh, Windows
endpoint, and Kali Linux) is a testing and demonstration environment. It
must not be represented as MFS's actual production infrastructure.

## 9. Preliminary Critical Business Dependencies

Based on the scenario, the following services may be important to
business operations:

1.  Customer account management.
2.  Financial transaction processing.
3.  Identity and access management.
4.  Network and internet connectivity.
5.  Database availability and integrity.
6.  Security monitoring and incident response.
7.  Backup, restoration, and disaster recovery.

The business impact of disruption to these services should be assessed
with process owners and supported by business impact analysis, recovery
requirements, and system dependency information where available.

## 10. Preliminary Areas for Further Audit Inquiry

The following are potential areas for inquiry, not confirmed risks,
control deficiencies, or audit findings:

-   How user access is requested, approved, provisioned, reviewed, and
    revoked.
-   How privileged accounts are restricted and monitored.
-   Whether security events are logged, monitored, and escalated.
-   How vulnerabilities and security patches are identified and
    addressed.
-   How sensitive data is classified, protected, retained, and disposed
    of.
-   Whether backups are protected and restoration is periodically
    tested.
-   How security incidents are reported, investigated, and resolved.
-   How third-party service providers are assessed and monitored.
-   How changes to critical applications and infrastructure are
    authorized and tested.

These topics will be evaluated during the appropriate risk assessment
and audit planning stages.

## 11. Regulatory and Framework Context to Validate

Depending on MFS's actual activities, data, and obligations, the audit
may need to consider:

-   Applicable U.S. financial services and consumer protection
    requirements.
-   The Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act (GLBA) and applicable Safeguards Rule
    requirements, if in scope.
-   PCI DSS, if payment card data or relevant payment environments are
    in scope.
-   NIST Cybersecurity Framework as a risk-management reference.
-   COBIT as a governance and management reference.
-   Applicable privacy, contractual, and data-protection obligations.

This section identifies matters for validation; it does not conclude
that every listed requirement applies to MFS.
